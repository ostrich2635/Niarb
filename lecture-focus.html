How this synthesizer keeps you focused.

Most people play white noise while studying to drown out barking dogs or roommate chatter. The problem is that human speech lives right in the middle of the audio spectrum—between 300 Hz and 3,000 Hz. Playing flat noise over a recorded lecture forces your brain to strain to decode consonants. Here is how NIARB solves this—and how to operate the console.

░ OPERATIONAL PROTOCOL [ CLICK ANY STEP TO LOCATE & TEST ON CONSOLE ]
STEP_01 // CALIBRATE VOICE

Match the Instructor's Pitch

Start your lecture video first. Engage NIARB and drag the red/blue node inside FIG_001 (around 850 Hz for deeper voices or 1650 Hz for higher voices) until speech cuts cleanly through the brown noise.

STEP_02 // SELECT BRAINWAVE

Pick Your Cognitive Band

Choose Alpha (10 Hz) for steady note-taking, Beta (18 Hz) for active math/coding problem sets, or Gamma (40 Hz) for intense memorization. Turn on Isochronic Mode if not wearing headphones.

STEP_03 // LOCK INTERVAL

Run 50-Minute Lecture Blocks

Start the 50:00 timer in SEC_03. Keep noise gain between 25% – 40%. When the timer finishes, NIARB gently ducks the noise floor and rings a harmonic bell so your ears never suffer shock.

[1] Formant Frequencies (600–2500 Hz) — The acoustic resonance peaks of the human vocal tract that distinguish vowels and consonants.
[2] Brownian Noise (1/f²) — Procedural noise that decreases in power by 6 dB per octave, removing harsh high-frequency hiss.
[3] Anti-Fatigue LFO (0.12 Hz) — A sub-audible oscillator that drifts the notch filter slightly so your auditory cortex does not habituate.
[4] Keyboard Shortcuts — Press Space to toggle audio, M to toggle the vocal carve filter, and V to switch FFT/Waveform view.
